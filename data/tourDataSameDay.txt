# Jama Masjid
The largest mosque in India, built between 1644 and 1658. It stands beautifully in Old Delhi, directly opposite the Red Fort, and is a significant landmark in the skyline.

# Red Fort (Closed on Monday)
Built by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan, this grand sandstone fort is famous for its Diwan-e-Am, Diwan-e-Khas, museums, and sound & light show. It is also the place from where the Prime Minister addresses the nation on Independence Day.

# Raj Ghat
The memorial of Mahatma Gandhi, inscribed with his last words "Hey Ram". The surrounding gardens also house Gandhi Memorial and Gandhi Smriti Museums.

# Jantar Mantar
Built by Maharaja Jai Singh II in 1724, this astronomical observatory features 13 architectural instruments designed for precise astronomical calculations.

# Lakshmi Narayan Birla Temple
A beautiful modern temple built in 1938 by B.D. Birla. It was one of Mahatma Gandhi's favorite places of worship.

# Indira Gandhi Memorial (Closed on Monday)
Former residence of India's Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, later converted into a museum after her assassination.

# Rashtrapati Bhavan (President House)
Originally built as the Viceroy's House by Sir Edwin Lutyens. This magnificent building with 340 rooms is the official residence of the President of India.

# Parliament House
Located near Vijay Chowk, this iconic circular structure houses India's Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, and Central Hall, symbolizing the world's largest democracy.

# India Gate
A 42-meter high war memorial designed by Lutyens in memory of soldiers who died in World War I. The eternal flame "Amar Jawan Jyoti" burns beneath the arch.

# Qutub Minar
A UNESCO World Heritage Site, this 72.5 m tall tower was started by Qutub-ud-din Aibak in 1199. Nearby stands the famous Iron Pillar which has resisted rust for centuries.

# Lotus Temple (Closed on Monday)
A Stunning Bahai House of Worship shaped like a lotus flower. Built with marble and open to people of all faiths for meditation and peace.

# Akshardham Temple (Closed on Monday)
A modern marvel of Indian architecture and spirituality. The temple complex features exhibitions, cultural performances, and an intricately carved central monument.

City Palace: Located near Hawa Mahal, the City Palace is the heart of Jaipur. It includes Chandra Mahal, Mubarak Mahal, and several other structures showcasing a blend of Rajput, Mughal, and European architecture. Today, a part of it houses the Maharaja Sawai Man Singh II Museum, displaying royal artifacts and artworks.

Hawa Mahal (Palace of Winds): Built in the 18th century by Sawai Pratap Singh, this five-storey monument allowed royal women to watch processions from behind latticed windows. Today, it houses a museum featuring paintings, coins, handicrafts, and sculptures.

Amber Palace & Fort: Situated 10 km from Jaipur, Amber Fort is a grand complex of palaces, gardens, and temples built over two centuries by Rajput rulers. Attractions include Diwan-e-Aam, Kali Temple, and Sheesh Mahal. Visitors can reach the fort by jeep or elephant ride.

Jantar Mantar: A UNESCO World Heritage Site, this observatory built by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II features massive stone and marble instruments, including the world's largest sundial.

Albert Hall Museum: Located in Ram Niwas Garden, this Indo-Saracenic museum was opened in 1887. It houses rare artifacts including an Egyptian mummy and the famous Persian Golden Carpet.

Nahargarh Fort: Standing atop the Aravalli hills, the fort offers panoramic views of Jaipur city. It was built as a defensive fort and now features a cafe for visitors.

Jaigarh Fort: Known for housing the world's largest cannon on wheels (Jaivana), this fort also features palaces, gardens, and an armory. It is one of the best-preserved military structures of medieval India.

Birla Mandir: A beautiful white marble temple dedicated to Lord Vishnu and Goddess Lakshmi, built by the Birla family at the foot of Moti Dungri hill.

Jal Mahal: A palace situated in the middle of Man Sagar Lake, known for its architectural beauty and serene surroundings.

Monkey Temple (Galta Ji): An ancient pilgrimage site located in the hills, featuring temples, natural springs, and a sacred water tank. It is also home to a large monkey population.

Other Attractions: Ishwar Lat, Gaitore Cenotaphs, Sisodia Rani Garden.

Delhi to Haridwar Rishikesh Tour
Duration: On your choice (1 / 2 / 3 Days Tour)

Destinations Covered: Delhi - Haridwar - Rishikesh - Delhi

Places to See in Haridwar
Har Ki Pauri: Constructed by King Vikramaditya in memory of his brother Bharthari, this sacred ghat is believed to bear Lord Vishnu's footprint. At twilight, the reflection of floral diyas in the Ganga creates a divine sight. It is regarded as the holiest place for bathing during the Kumbh and Ardh Kumbh festivals.

Sapt Rishi Ashram & Sapt Sarovar: According to legend, seven sages (Saptarishis) meditated here. To avoid disturbing them, the Ganga split into seven streams (Sapt Sarovar).

Mansa Devi Temple: Located atop Bilwa Parvat, the temple is dedicated to Goddess Mansa Devi. A ropeway with colorful cable cars takes devotees to the hilltop shrine.

Chandi Devi Temple: Built in 1929 by the King of Kashmir, this temple sits on Neel Parvat. It is believed that Adi Shankaracharya installed the main deity in the 8th century. A trek or ropeway takes you to the temple.

Maya Devi Temple: One of the Siddh Peethas, this ancient temple is dedicated to Goddess Maya Devi, where the heart and navel of Goddess Sati are believed to have fallen.

Daksha Mahadev Temple: Situated in Kankhal, this temple is linked with the legend of King Daksha, father of Sati. The temple marks the spot where Sati immolated herself during Daksha's yagya.

Other Attractions: Kanva Rishi Ashram, Bilkeshwar Mahadev, Pawan Dham, Bhooma Niketan, Vaishno Devi Temple, Bharat Mata Mandir, Doodhadhari Temple, Shahi Gurudwara, Jairam Ashram.

Places to See in Rishikesh
Bharat Mandir: The oldest temple in Rishikesh near Triveni Ghat, dedicated to Bharat, Lord Ram's younger brother.

Kailash Niketan Temple: Located at Laxman Jhula, this 13-storey temple has sculptures of numerous Hindu deities.

Satya Narayan Temple: Situated enroute to Rishikesh, this temple enshrines Lord Satyanarayan and is a popular halt for devotees.

Shatrughan Temple: An ancient temple dedicated to Shatrughan, Lord Ram's brother, located 5 km from Rishikesh.

Neelkanth Mahadev Temple: Located on a hill across the Ganga, this temple marks the spot where Lord Shiva drank the poison from the Samudra Manthan, turning his throat blue.

Other Attractions: Ram Jhula and Laxman Jhula, iconic suspension bridges over the Ganga.
